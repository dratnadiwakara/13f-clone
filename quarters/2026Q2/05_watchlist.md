# 2026Q2 watchlist — near-miss names worth a look

Subjective assessment by Claude Code on 2026-08-20. Not a screen, not reproducible; re-running may produce a different list. Not part of the portfolio. Data is PROVISIONAL (individual EDGAR filings; SEC bulk not yet published).

Pool: 36 names bought by tracked managers at 1–10% conviction, all quant-pass and in the better half of the Greenblatt universe (`05_watchlist_data.csv`). Picks are ranked in the order to look at them as long-term investments — durability of the franchise first, then price paid, then manager conviction.

## Picks (in order)

**1. V — Visa (mf 0.23)**
The most durable franchise on the list: payments network with ROIC ex-goodwill 135%, 70% gross margin, essentially no net debt, and a moat measured in decades. Pershing 6.2% and AltaRock 2.7% this quarter, after Baupost bought in Q1 — three quality-focused shops in two quarters. Perennially "fairly priced" (EV/EBIT 24); ranked first because for a long horizon the odds of permanent capital loss are the lowest here. Regulatory interchange action is the one structural risk.

**2. SPGI — S&P Global (mf 0.23)**
The strongest conviction momentum in the book: Dorsey now at 8.5% (up from 7.5% last quarter, 1.5 points from HC) and Pershing Square building a new 5.8% stake alongside. Ratings/index duopoly, 70% gross margin, 78% ROIC ex-goodwill — franchise quality just behind Visa's, at the same kind of fair-not-cheap price (EV/EBIT 26). If either manager keeps adding, the most likely new HC name of 2026Q3.

**3. MSI — Motorola Solutions (mf 0.25)**
Newbrook at 8.1%, accumulating since 2025Q4 — a quiet single-manager campaign within two points of HC. Public-safety communications monopoly: 53% gross margin, ROIC ex-goodwill 54%, sticky multi-decade government demand. Leverage (2.5× EBITDA) is the one blemish and why it sits below the two networks. The most likely "crosses 10% unnoticed" candidate.

**4. NFLX — Netflix (mf 0.28)**
Three separate managers this quarter: Pershing Square 5.2% (new), plus Terry Smith at ~7.2% combined across his two Fundsmith filing entities — second consecutive quarter of broad accumulation. Streaming war largely won, revenue +13%/yr, economics improving yearly. EV/EBIT 29 makes it a fair-price compounder; content-spend discipline is what to watch over a decade.

**5. LULU — Lululemon (mf 0.10)**
The value case: EV/EBIT 9 and P/E 13 for a debt-free business still earning 58% 3-yr ROIC on 61% gross margin. RWWM adding a second quarter (2.2%). Ranked mid-list because the long-term question is brand durability — the market is pricing athleisure fade, and conviction is thin. Stabilizing comps or a second manager would move it up.

**6. IDCC — InterDigital (mf 0.17)**
Shannon River 3.3%, new. Wireless-patent licensing: 126% ROIC, 65% gross margin, net cash, revenue +22%/yr, EV/EBIT 16. Economics are extreme, but revenue is litigation-driven and lumpy, and patent portfolios age — a fine business to study, a harder one to hold for ten years.

**7. GTX — Garrett Motion (mf 0.06)**
Cheapest quality name in the whole ranked universe (EV/EBIT 9, 80% 3-yr ROIC), ACK Asset Management at 6.3%. Ranked last deliberately: turbochargers are an internal-combustion product with flat revenue — a melting ice cube priced as one. Only worth long-term money if you believe the hybrid-adoption offset; a leg down in revenue CAGR (already −0.2%) settles it.

## Ignore despite the numbers

- **GPC** — P/E of 257 is an earnings-trough artifact; the rest of the row is ordinary.
- **CELH** — negative 3-yr ROIC; the growth story rests entirely on one year.
- **NVDA / AVGO / TSM** — mega-cap semis at 1–7% weights; nothing contrarian, and TSM already fell to the price cap as an HC name in Q1.
- **BRK.B** — mf 0.09 is a conglomerate-accounting illusion; insurance float distorts every ratio here.
