# 2026Q3 watchlist: near-miss names worth a look

Subjective assessment by Claude Code on 2026-11-16. Not a screen, not reproducible; re-running may produce a different list. Not part of the portfolio. Data is PROVISIONAL (individual EDGAR 13F-HR filings, 39 of 39 expected managers; SEC bulk data not yet published).

The pool is 35 names that tracked managers bought at 1% to 10% conviction. All pass the quant screen and rank in the better half of the magic-formula universe (`05_watchlist_data.csv`). I rank the picks in the order to look at them as long-term investments: durability of the franchise first, then the price paid, then manager conviction.

## Picks (in order)

**1. V, Visa (mf 0.23)**
Lone Pine holds 5.2%, Thames 1.9% and Fundsmith 1.0%, so three managers bought in the window. Visa earns a 145% ROIC excluding goodwill on a 70% gross margin, with debt near zero (0.1x EBITDA). It ranks first because a payments network has the lowest chance of permanent capital loss over ten years. The price is fair, not cheap (EV/EBIT 22). Regulatory action on interchange fees is the main risk.

**2. MSFT, Microsoft (mf 0.27)**
Five managers bought: AltaRock 7.9%, Whale Rock 4.5%, Thames 3.2%, Light Street 2.4% and Columbus Hill 1.5%. That is the broadest buying in the pool. Gross margin is 80% and revenue grew 16% a year over three years. At EV/EBIT 18 it is cheaper than Visa or S&P Global. It ranks second because the cloud and software franchise is about as durable as Visa's, but heavy AI capital spending makes future returns on capital less certain. MSFT was an HC name in 2026Q2 and was cut only at rank 8.

**3. SPGI, S&P Global (mf 0.22)**
Dorsey is at 9.1% conviction, 0.9 points below the HC threshold, and has added for three quarters. Pershing Square also holds 3.3%. Credit ratings and indices are close to a duopoly, with a 70% gross margin and a 78% ROIC excluding goodwill. It ranks below Microsoft on price (EV/EBIT 26) and on leverage (1.5x EBITDA). If Dorsey adds again, SPGI is the most likely new HC name in 2026Q4.

**4. META, Meta Platforms (mf 0.35)**
Six managers bought, more than any other name: CAS 6.0%, Lone Pine 5.9%, Light Street 5.9%, Whale Rock 5.4%, Dorsey 2.1% and SRS 1.7%. Gross margin is 91%, revenue grew 20% a year, and EV/EBIT is 20. META was in the 2026Q2 portfolio and fell out of the HC set this quarter only because Pershing's weight was diluted (see the note below). It ranks fourth because advertising revenue is more cyclical than payments or ratings, and capital spending is high.

**5. BKNG, Booking Holdings (mf 0.17)**
Dorsey holds 9.2% conviction, 0.8 points below HC. BKNG was in the 2026Q2 portfolio at 13.8% conviction. Its weight in Dorsey's book fell from 8.5% to 7.9% this quarter, while Dorsey opened VEEV and TYL. Revenue grew 16% a year, debt is low (0.2x EBITDA), and EV/EBIT is 18. Its negative 3-year ROIC comes from negative book equity after buybacks, not from weak operations. A shift of travel search to AI assistants is the risk to watch.

**6. MELI, MercadoLibre (mf 0.44)**
3G Capital holds 9.4% conviction and Lone Pine 1.9%. Revenue grew 40% a year over three years, and the company holds net cash. The price is high (EV/EBIT 30, P/E 51), and the credit book makes the 165% 3-year ROIC hard to read. It ranks sixth because Latin American e-commerce and payments are a durable position, but the price leaves little room for error. One more purchase by 3G would make it an HC name.

**7. LULU, Lululemon (mf 0.10)**
RWWM has added for a third quarter, now at 3.7%. This is the value case: EV/EBIT 9 and P/E 13 for a debt-free business with a 58% 3-year ROIC and a 61% gross margin. It ranks low because the market is pricing a fade in the brand, and only one manager is buying. Stable US same-store sales or a second buyer would move it up.

**8. YUM, Yum! Brands (mf 0.24)**
Fundsmith files under two entities, holding 4.5% and 3.9%. Together that is 8.4% from one manager, Terry Smith. The franchise model earns a 63% 3-year ROIC with little capital, and EV/EBIT is 20. It ranks last because of leverage at 4.2x EBITDA and 6% revenue growth.

## Close to the HC threshold but not picked

- **CVNA** (RV Capital, 9.8%): EV/EBIT 33 on a 21% gross margin, with a 2022 debt crisis in the company's recent past. The margin of safety is thin.
- **MU** (Appaloosa, 9.1%): EV/EBIT 10, but earnings move with memory prices. Revenue doubled in a year, so the low multiple is on cyclical earnings.
- **MTZ** (Newbrook, 8.9%): an infrastructure contractor with a 13% gross margin, ranked at mf 0.50.

## Ignore despite the numbers

- **KSPI, KNSL, KKR**: a bank-like fintech, an insurer and an asset manager. ROC of 5x to 7x is an artifact of financial balance sheets, not a sign of quality.
- **CDLR** (Cadeler, Folketrygdfondet 7.1%): it builds offshore wind installation vessels. FCF/NI is -3.7 because of new-build capital spending, and revenue depends on the wind project cycle.
- **ATAT**: the -2.9 3-year ROIC is a book equity artifact. Read the filings before trusting any ratio in this row.
- **NVDA, AVGO, AMAT, LRCX, ASML**: mega-cap semiconductor names at 1% to 5% weights. They show crowd positioning, not a contrarian view.

## Harsh-cut and data notes (from the funnel)

- **Pershing Square dilution.** Pershing's 2026Q3 13F shows "PERSHING SQUARE INC" at 33.5% of its reported portfolio, which looks like a stake in its own listed vehicle. That position doubles as the HC row PS (35.1%, cut by the screen). It also shrinks every other Pershing weight by about a third: UBER fell from 12.7% to 7.6%, and MSFT from 11.9% to 6.8%. That is why UBER and META left the HC set. A manager holding its own vehicle is not a stock-picking signal. This deserves a decision before the portfolio is acted on.
- **TSM** (3G, 10.6%) passed every test but was blocked by the macro gate: market cap $1,937bn against a $1,651bn limit at S&P PE 25.3. Under the backfill rule adopted on 2026-11-16, CLS (Shannon River, 10.4%, mf 0.41) takes the slot, so the portfolio holds 7 names.
- **AMZN** (3G 19.5%, Lone Pine 10.1%) and **VEEV** (Dorsey 14.1%, new) are HC and pass the screen, but they rank 0.50 and 0.53 and fall outside the top 7.
- **TEVA** (Lingotto, 12.1%, new) ranks 0.10, which would place it second, but it fails cash conversion.
