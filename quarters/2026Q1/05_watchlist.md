# 2026Q1 watchlist — near-miss names worth a look

Subjective assessment by Claude Code on 2026-08-20. Not a screen, not reproducible; re-running may produce a different list. Not part of the portfolio.

Pool: 39 names bought by tracked managers at 1–10% conviction, all quant-pass and in the better half of the Greenblatt universe (`05_watchlist_data.csv`). Picks are ranked in the order to look at them as long-term investments — durability of the franchise first, then price paid, then manager conviction.

## Picks (in order)

**1. SPGI — S&P Global (mf 0.23)**
The most durable business in the pool: a ratings/index duopoly with 70% gross margin and 78% ROIC ex-goodwill, and the kind of pricing power that compounds for decades. Dorsey at 7.5% — a concentrated franchise investor three points from HC — plus a small Li Lu position. The price is the objection (EV/EBIT 26, P/E 35); this is quality-at-a-fair-price, not a bargain. Ranked first because for a long horizon the business risk is the lowest here.

**2. UBER — Uber Technologies (mf 0.30)**
Appaloosa at 7.5% and Dorsey at 6.2% — two managers independently near the threshold, Appaloosa building since 2025Q1. Revenue compounding at 18%/yr, now solidly profitable (3-yr pre-tax ROIC 14% and rising fast off a low base), fundamentals P/E ~17 — the best growth-per-dollar in the pool. Ranked behind SPGI only because the network-effect moat is younger and competition (autonomy) is a live long-term question. What would change the view: managers trimming, or FCF conversion (0.92) deteriorating.

**3. FICO — Fair Isaac (mf 0.30)**
Akre at 7.9% — the closest conviction miss in the pool. Monopoly-like credit-score franchise: 83% gross margin, ROIC ex-goodwill 177%, decades of pricing power ahead. EV/EBIT 41 even after the de-rating — you are paying up, which is why it sits third rather than first. A manager known for never selling is buying the dip; regulatory pressure on score pricing is the risk that would change the assessment.

**4. NFLX — Netflix (mf 0.28)**
Two managers near-miss: Greenlea Lane 7.8%, Whale Rock 4.5%, first buys back in 2025Q2 — a slow, persistent campaign. Revenue +13%/yr, operating economics improving every year, and the streaming war largely won. EV/EBIT 29 and FCF/NI 0.93 make it a fair-price compounder, not a bargain; content-spend discipline is what to watch.

**5. CROX — Crocs (mf 0.03)**
The statistical standout: EV/EBIT 6.4 on 59% gross margin and 30% ROIC ex-goodwill. Ranked fifth despite being cheapest because the long-term question is the brand itself — the market is pricing HEYDUDE-style decay, and only Li Lu at 2.3% is on the other side. The negative reported P/E is a non-cash impairment (clean earnings fine, FCF/NI 1.44). A second manager or insider buying would move it up this list.

**6. WFRD — Weatherford International (mf 0.14)**
Sachem Head (activist) at 5.8%, new this quarter. Post-restructuring oilfield services: net debt under 0.6× EBITDA, 41% pre-tax ROIC, EV/EBIT 8. Last because it is the least "own for a decade" name here — cyclical end-market, thesis is balance-sheet repair plus activist pressure, not franchise. Watch for Sachem Head pushing past 10%.

## Ignore despite the numbers

- **MGNI** — FCF/NI of 67× is a denominator artifact (near-zero net income), not cash gushing.
- **CGAU** — 170× interest cover and negative debt/EBITDA are small-miner balance-sheet quirks; single 1.2% position.
- **AAPL** — Whale Rock at 2.2% is noise for a mega-cap; nothing contrarian here.
